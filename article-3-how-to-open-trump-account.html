How to Open a Trump Account for Your Child: The 2026 Step-by-Step

Quick answer: your child most likely already has a Trump Account. Since auto-enrollment finished on October 1, 2026, "opening" one now means claiming it. Claiming is also what puts the one-time $1,000 seed deposit in motion for eligible babies. Here's exactly how to do it.

First, what changed in October 2026

Trump Accounts were created by the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (Internal Revenue Code §530A) and launched on July 4, 2026. Under Treasury's regulations, the government then auto-created accounts for every child under 18 with a Social Security number, more than 60 million of them. The White House reported on October 8 that nearly 70 million accounts exist in total, with $4.5 billion already deposited.

An auto-created account is not the same as a claimed one. Until a parent or guardian claims the account, it just sits there. And for babies born in the pilot window, the $1,000 does not go in until you claim and elect it.

How to claim your child's Trump Account, step by step

  1. Go to the official site: trumpaccounts.gov. That's the real one. It ends in .gov. Lookalike sites and phishing pages are circulating, so type the address yourself instead of tapping a link from a random post or text.
  2. Start the claim as the parent or legal guardian. You'll verify your identity and your child's.
  3. Enter your child's details exactly as they appear in Social Security records. This is the step that trips up the most families: if the name or SSN you type doesn't match the Social Security Administration's records character for character, the claim fails with an "unable to process" message. Check the Social Security card, not the birth announcement.
  4. Elect the $1,000 pilot deposit if your child qualifies. The one-time $1,000 seed is for U.S.-citizen children with an SSN born between January 1, 2025 and December 31, 2028. There is no income limit on it, and it is not a "match." You don't have to deposit anything to receive it.
  5. Finish activation and save your confirmation. Once claimed, you can see the account, set up contributions, and check whether any extra deposits landed in it, like the Dell philanthropic $250 that went automatically to roughly 25 million children born 2016 to 2024 in qualifying lower-income ZIP codes.

Newborn? You may need to wait a few weeks. A baby has to have an SSN before an account can be claimed, and a newborn's SSN typically takes a few weeks to arrive after the birth is registered. There's no rush penalty: claiming later doesn't shrink the $1,000.

The paper route: IRS Form 4547

If you'd rather not use the website, you can make the election on IRS Form 4547 (the Trump Account election form), filed on paper or with your tax return. It does the same job as the online claim-and-elect. It's just slower. Either way, the election happens through the IRS/Treasury channel. It never happens at a bank or brokerage: if a website or salesperson offers to "open one for you" outside trumpaccounts.gov or Form 4547, walk away.

After you've claimed it

  • Contributions: Family and friends can contribute up to $5,000 per year per child, total. Contributions are not tax-deductible.
  • Employer contributions: An employer can contribute up to $2,500 a year, excluded from the employee's income, but it counts inside the same $5,000 cap. It isn't extra room on top, and it isn't a match.
  • Investing: Money goes into diversified, low-cost U.S. index funds. It's an invested account, not a savings account, so the value moves with the market.
  • Withdrawals: There are none during the growth period. At 18, the account is treated much like a traditional IRA, tax-deferred, not tax-free.
  • One date to know: December 31, 2026 is the last day contributions count for 2026. There's no prior-year catch-up window, so if contributing this year matters to your family, don't let it slide into January.

Common errors (and what they actually mean)

  • "Login not working / unable to process." Almost always a name or SSN mismatch with Social Security records, or the account hasn't been activated yet. Re-check the exact spelling on the Social Security card and try again.
  • "My baby isn't found." If your newborn's SSN was issued in the last few weeks, the records may not have synced yet. Wait and retry.
  • "I tried to open one at my bank/brokerage." They can't. Elections run only through trumpaccounts.gov or Form 4547.
  • "Someone said there's a June 30 deadline." There isn't. That date doesn't exist in the IRS or Treasury rules. The real dates are the December 31, 2028 end of the $1,000 birth window and the December 31 annual contribution cutoff.

FAQ

When can I open a Trump Account?

Right now. Auto-enrollment is complete, so for most families it's a claim, not an application. You can do it today at trumpaccounts.gov or by filing Form 4547.

Can I open one before my baby's SSN arrives?

No. An SSN is required. Newborn SSNs usually arrive within a few weeks of birth registration. Your baby's eligibility doesn't expire while you wait.

Does my older child get the $1,000?

No. Children born before 2025 can have a Trump Account (any child under 18 with an SSN can), but the $1,000 seed is only for U.S.-citizen children born January 1, 2025 through December 31, 2028. Older kids may have received the separate Dell $250 deposit if they were born 2016 to 2024 and live in a qualifying ZIP. That's automatic, no application.

Is there a deadline to open or claim?

There's no deadline to claim an existing account, but two dates matter: contributions for 2026 stop counting after December 31, 2026, and babies born after December 31, 2028 fall outside the $1,000 pilot window.
